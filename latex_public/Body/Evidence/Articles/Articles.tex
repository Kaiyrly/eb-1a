\noindent\underline{Scientific paper: ``Geonomics: address-registry maintenance and population heat-map analytics} \\
\underline{for smart-city operations''} \\
(A.B. Taximov, A.A. Beisenbayev), published in: \emph{Engineering Bulletin of the Don}, No. 9, 2024.

\emph{Engineering Bulletin of the Don} is a VAK-listed, peer-reviewed electronic scientific journal published by the North-Caucasus Research Center of Southern Federal University.

The article introduces the Geonomics system and proposes an integrated methodological and algorithmic framework for maintaining up-to-date population and address data in large urban environments. The proposed model combines address registries, digital cartography, and government databases into a unified analytical platform that reduces inconsistencies in citizen registration data, minimizes manual errors, and improves the accuracy of territorial population analysis. The paper presents original algorithms for data standardization, polygon-based spatial analysis, automated voter-to-address linkage, and population heat-map generation, enabling reliable association of residents with administrative units and supporting data-driven urban planning and public service allocation.
In addition, the article proposes a methodology for automated synchronization of residency data through structured integration with government registries, significantly improving data timeliness and consistency. The described geospatial subsystems demonstrate practical effectiveness in planning social infrastructure such as schools and hospitals, contributing to more efficient use of municipal resources and improved service accessibility.

(A.B. Taximov, A.A. Beisenbayev), published in: \emph{Innovations and Investments}, No. 11, 2023.

\emph{Innovations and Investments} is a VAK-listed, peer-reviewed journal focused on economics, management, and innovation.

The article examines the role of open data in smart city governance and proposes a conceptual and architectural model for transitioning from passive data publication to an integrated, API-driven open data ecosystem. The proposed approach addresses key limitations of traditional open data portals, including data fragmentation, low reuse, and limited civic engagement.
The article introduces an integrated open data architecture that consolidates heterogeneous datasets using ETL-based processing, standardized formats, and controlled API access, enabling secure and scalable data reuse while ensuring regulatory compliance. In addition, it proposes a citizen-centered governance framework that incorporates feedback mechanisms and dataset interaction, demonstrating how open data can support transparency, civic participation, and participatory decision making in smart city management.
